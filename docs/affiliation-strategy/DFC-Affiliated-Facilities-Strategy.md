# DFC Affiliated Facilities — Strategy & Platform Build Plan

*Internal strategy memo · Doctors Foundation for Care · Draft v1 · August 2026*

---

## 1. The thesis in one paragraph

A facility directory is not defensible — anyone can list hospitals, and several already do. What only DFC can credibly produce is **a physician-conferred trust seal**: a mark that says *"a body of credentialed Nigerian physicians has vetted this facility and stands behind it."* Nigeria has no shortage of hospitals and no working signal of which ones are sound. DFC owns the one asset that mints that signal — professional, peer-based legitimacy. So the product is **the seal and the network it plugs into, not the page**. We should build a tiered, apply-and-confer accreditation program for Nigeria-based facilities, seed it invite-only with reputable member-owned practices, and wire it directly into DFC's clinical services (second opinion, diagnostics, referrals) so affiliation delivers *patient throughput*, not just a logo.

The single most important design rule: **affiliation must be earned and revocable, never self-claimed.** The moment a member can "forge and claim" the badge, the seal is worthless and becomes a liability. The rigor *is* the moat.

---

## 2. Why now — the market and the tailwind

**The outflow we are recapturing.** Nigerians spend heavily on care abroad. The Central Bank's recorded health-related travel allowance alone was **~$684.7m in 2025** (up 6.5% YoY from ~$643m in 2024), and this undercounts reality — self-funded and unrecorded spend push commonly cited totals to **$1.6bn+**, with foreign travel for *education + medical* combined estimated near **$6bn**. Top destinations: **India (42.7%), USA (15.6%), UAE (11.3%), UK (10.8%), Saudi Arabia (6.7%)**. Every naira of that is a vote of no-confidence in domestic facilities' *trustworthiness*, not always their capability. Trust is the bottleneck — which is exactly what a seal addresses.

**The quality-signaling gap.** Nigerian facility regulation is registration-level, not quality-differentiating. Lagos HEFAMAA registration is mandatory (₦100k fine for non-compliance) with twice-yearly monitoring, but it certifies *existence and minimum standards*, not excellence, and it is state-bound. MDCN, NHIA, PCN, NAFDAC each license a slice. **Nothing tells a diaspora family "this specific hospital in Enugu is one you can trust with your mother."** That gap is the product opportunity.

**The government tailwind.** The Federal Government's **Diaspora Health Impact Initiative (DHII, 2026)** is explicitly courting **50,000+ diaspora Nigerian doctors**, promising easier licensing, locum arrangements, customs/visa waivers, a **Diaspora Investment Fund**, a **National Diaspora Health Corps**, and telemedicine links between diaspora specialists and home patients. **DFC is already the organized, credentialed body these campaigns are trying to assemble.** We should position DFC Affiliated Facilities as the private-sector, physician-governed complement to DHII — and move before a government or commercial actor builds a weaker version of the same seal.

---

## 3. Where the edge is, and why it holds

Three layers, only the first of which a directory competitor can copy:

1. **The trust seal** — "DFC Affiliated Facility," peer-verified, publicly displayed, revocable.
2. **The diaspora referral rail** — DFC members abroad refer family and patients to affiliated home facilities with confidence. This is the medical-tourism-*repatriation* engine. No commercial directory has a captive, credentialed referral base with patients' trust; DFC does, by definition.
3. **Plug-in to DFC clinical machinery** — affiliated facilities become the delivery arm for Second Opinion follow-through, the diagnostic network, ACO, and structured EMR. Affiliation buys *throughput and capability*, not just a badge.

Layers 2 and 3 are the moat. A listings competitor (or an HMO network) can build layer 1's page; it cannot manufacture a physician body with referral trust, and it cannot bundle DFC's clinical services. **Defensibility = authority to confer trust × captive referral demand × service integration.**

## 4. What to borrow from the people who already do this

| Body | Model | What it proves / what to take |
|---|---|---|
| **SafeCare (PharmAccess + JCI + COHSASA)** | ISQua-accredited standards built *for sub-Saharan Africa*; **824 criteria across 13 service areas**; **stepwise certification** (improve → get certified at a level → progress toward full accreditation); ~300 facilities across Nigeria, Kenya, Tanzania, Ghana, Namibia since 2011; links quality data to financing. | The closest working analog, already in Nigeria. **Take:** the stepwise/graded model (facilities climb, not pass/fail), the LMIC-appropriate standards, and the quality-data-to-financing link. Do **not** reinvent standards — license or align to SafeCare-type criteria and layer DFC's *physician endorsement + referral network* on top as the differentiator. |
| **COHSASA** | The only ISQua-accredited African accreditor; web self-evaluation portal (CoQIS) → external survey by independent panellists → accreditation; **18–24 months** for a public hospital; **Graded Recognition Programme** certifies progressive compliance levels for under-resourced facilities. | **Take:** independent surveyors (separation between those who help and those who judge — critical for integrity), the self-assessment portal, and graded recognition so early-stage facilities have a ladder. |
| **JCI** | Global gold-standard hospital accreditation; the aspirational tier, expensive, used by medical-tourism destinations. | **Take:** the "Centre of Excellence" aspiration as our top tier and a co-brand/pathway target for elite affiliates. |
| **NABH (India)** | National accreditation tied to money: **NABH facilities get expedited PM-JAY (national insurance) empanelment + a 10–15% package-rate premium**, insurer preference, **up to ~30% patient-footfall increase**, ~40% lower litigation risk; cost ₹50L–1.5cr for a 100-bed hospital; ROI in 2–3 years. | The **willingness-to-pay proof**. Accreditation pays for itself when it is tied to *volume and premium pricing*. Our equivalents of PM-JAY empanelment are **NHIA/HMO empanelment support + the DFC diaspora referral rail**. |
| **HEFAMAA / MDCN / NHIA** | Statutory registration and licensing; mandatory, minimum-standard, fragmented by state/profession. | Defines the floor we sit **above**. We are not a regulator; we are a *trust and referral overlay*. Align with (never contradict) statutory status — in fact require valid HEFAMAA/MDCN standing as an entry gate. |

**Competitive/adjacent read:** Reliance Health (~$40m Series B, General Atlantic) runs HMO + provider network + telemedicine + clinics; Vezeeta, Helium Health, LifeBank occupy booking/records/logistics. All are commercial and none confer *physician-peer trust*. Our seal is orthogonal to and potentially *partnerable* with them (an HMO would value a physician-vetted quality tier for its network).

## 5. The DFC model — tiers, standards, governance

### Tiering (a prestige ladder people climb, revocable at every rung)

| Tier | Name | Bar | Verification depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (gate) | *Eligible* | Valid CAC + HEFAMAA/state registration + MDCN-licensed medical lead + a DFC member with a bona-fide ownership/clinical stake | Documentary + registry check |
| 1 | **DFC Member Practice** | Owned/led by a DFC member in good standing; identity + role verified | Identity & ownership verification (mirrors existing member credential flow) |
| 2 | **DFC Affiliated Facility** | Meets a published standards checklist (safety, staffing, infection control, records, patient rights); **independent peer survey**; time-bound (e.g. 2 yrs), renewable | On-site or evidenced survey by an independent DFC assessor panel |
| 3 | **DFC Centre of Excellence** | Specialty designation (e.g. cardiac, oncology, maternal) with outcome reporting; deep vetting; pathway toward JCI/SafeCare full accreditation | Specialty committee + outcomes audit |

Start everyone at the gate; **only invite the seed cohort straight into Tier 2** (see §8). Scarcity first.

### Standards
Do **not** write standards from scratch. Adopt/align to a SafeCare-style, LMIC-appropriate criteria set (safety, human resources, patient rights & access, risk management, medication management, records, support services). Publish the checklist openly — transparency is part of the trust. DFC's *addition* is the **physician endorsement, the referral network, and the service integration**, not a novel rulebook.

### Governance (this is what protects the brand)
- An **Accreditation & Standards Committee** (DFC physicians, drawn from EXCO/committees already in the platform) owns criteria, confers, and revokes.
- **Separation of duties:** whoever helps a facility improve must not be the one who surveys/approves it (COHSASA principle).
- **Conflict-of-interest rules:** members own these facilities. Owners recuse from their own reviews; surveys are done by independent panels; disclosures are logged. Without this, the whole thing is compromised.
- **Time-bound, revocable status** with periodic re-audit, a **complaints/sanctions process**, and public status (Active / Under Review / Suspended / Revoked).

## 6. How we "exploit" it for value (leverage & monetization)

**Direct revenue**
- **Annual affiliation/accreditation fee**, tiered (Tier 1 nominal → Tier 3 premium). NABH shows facilities pay real money when the seal drives volume.
- **Assessment/survey fees** (one-off per cycle) — cost-recovery + margin on the survey.
- **Premium showcase / microsite** on the DFC platform (photos, specialties, equipment, the endorsing member, outcomes).
- **Quality-improvement + CME services** — the sticky, mission-aligned line: we *help* facilities reach the standard (gap assessments, training, mock surveys). Recurring, defensible, and it improves the very facilities we vouch for.
- **Referral facilitation** — a fee or subscription on the diaspora-to-home referral rail; second-opinion and diagnostic throughput routed to affiliates.

**Indirect / strategic leverage**
- **HMO / NHIA partnerships:** sell the vetted tier as a preferred-provider quality layer to insurers (the NABH-PM-JAY analog). Insurers gain a trusted network; facilities gain empanelment; DFC monetizes the bridge.
- **Medical-tourism repatriation:** market DFC Centres of Excellence to the diaspora as credible home alternatives to India/UK for defined procedures — recapturing a slice of the $0.7–1.6bn outflow.
- **Corporate & embassy contracts:** vetted-facility networks for employers and missions.
- **Data:** aggregate (anonymized) quality/outcome data becomes an asset for research, policy, and financing partners.

**The flywheel (engineer this deliberately):**
diaspora physician trusts the seal → refers family/patients to an affiliated facility → facility gains volume + revenue → facility pays for/renews affiliation and climbs tiers → seal accrues prestige → more reputable facilities apply → more diaspora members refer. Seed the *supply* side first (curated facilities) so early referrers meet quality; seed *demand* via the existing DFC diaspora membership.

## 7. How it can be exploited *against us* — integrity, fraud & liability (the core risk)

This section is the other reading of "how can this be exploited," and it is the make-or-break.

| Abuse / risk | How it happens | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-claimed / purchased seal** | A member drapes the DFC name over a weak facility, or "claims" affiliation with no verification | **Apply-and-confer only.** No self-service badge. Seal issued by committee after independent survey; every affiliate has a public, verifiable status page with issue/expiry date and a unique registry ID. |
| **Badge misuse after revocation / by non-affiliates** | Expired or fake facilities keep displaying the mark | Central **public registry** as the single source of truth (verify by ID/QR); trademark the seal; takedown + naming policy; the badge is a live lookup, not a static image. |
| **Conflict of interest** | Member-owners influence their own facility's review | Owner recusal, independent panels, logged disclosures, rotation of assessors. |
| **"Pay-to-pass"/capture** | Fees create pressure to approve | Separate the money (membership/fees) from the verdict (committee); publish pass/fail criteria; sanctions for assessors who breach. |
| **Medico-legal liability** | An affiliated facility harms a patient; DFC is seen to have vouched | Frame the seal explicitly as a **"network standard, not a clinical guarantee."** Clear disclaimers; professional indemnity; require affiliates to carry insurance; a complaints and rapid-suspension process; document that DFC accredits *systems and standards*, not individual clinical outcomes. Legal review before launch. |
| **Standard erosion at scale** | Growth pressure lowers the bar | Hard-cap early cohorts; keep Tier 2+ deliberately scarce; annual re-audit; publish revocations (revoking loudly *increases* trust). |
| **Reputational contagion** | One scandal at a "DFC" facility damages the institution | Fast, transparent suspension; crisis protocol; the tiering limits blast radius (a Tier-1 identity badge ≠ a Tier-3 clinical endorsement). |

**Bottom line:** every euro of value in §6 is borrowed against the integrity in §7. Verification rigor, independence, revocability, and clear liability framing are not overhead — they are the product.

## 8. Go-to-market (phased)

- **Phase 0 — Seed (invite-only, this cohort).** Hand-pick 8–15 genuinely reputable member-owned facilities. Grant Tier 2 after a real (even if expedited) survey. Their credibility bootstraps the seal's prestige. Publish them as founding affiliates.
- **Phase 1 — Rail.** Turn on diaspora referral + second-opinion/diagnostic routing to the seed cohort so early affiliates get *volume*, proving ROI.
- **Phase 2 — Open applications + standards.** Publish the checklist, open Tier 0→2 applications, stand up the independent assessor panel and CME/QI services.
- **Phase 3 — Monetize the bridges.** HMO/NHIA preferred-network deals, medical-tourism repatriation marketing, Centres of Excellence, embassy/corporate contracts.

Governance, registry, and liability framing ship in **Phase 0** — before the first badge is displayed.

## 9. Platform build plan (grounded in the current codebase)

The good news: this reuses patterns DFC already has. The member flow already models **apply → PENDING → verified** (`DFCMember.status`, `MedicalCredential` + `VerificationStatus`), the **secretariat verification queue** (`SecretariatTicket`), and a **public directory** (`/api/public/doctors`, the specialists page). Facilities are the same shape applied to organizations instead of people.

### 9.1 Data model (new Prisma models)
- **`Facility`** — `id, slug (unique), name, type (HOSPITAL|CLINIC|DIAGNOSTIC|SPECIALTY_CENTRE), ownerUserId (→ DFC member), state, city, address, description, logo, phone, email, website, cacNumber, hefamaaNumber, mdcnLeadLicence, coordinates`.
- **`FacilityAffiliation`** — `facilityId, tier (MEMBER_PRACTICE|AFFILIATED|CENTRE_OF_EXCELLENCE), status (ELIGIBLE|APPLIED|UNDER_REVIEW|ACTIVE|SUSPENDED|REVOKED|EXPIRED), registryId (public unique), issuedAt, expiresAt, conferredByCommitteeId, revokedReason`.
- **`FacilityStandardResult`** — per-criterion survey scoring (mirrors `MedicalCredential`), the audit trail.
- **`FacilityMedia`**, **`FacilityService`** (specialties/procedures offered), **`FacilitySurvey`** (assessor, date, verdict, independence/COI attestation), **`FacilityReferral`** (links to `SecondOpinionCase` / diagnostics for the rail + attribution).
- Reuse **`Specialty`** for facility services; reuse **`SecretariatTicket`** for the review workflow queue.

### 9.2 Workflows
- **Apply-and-confer:** member submits facility + evidence → creates a review ticket → independent assessor panel surveys → Standards Committee confers/denies tier → status flips to `ACTIVE` with `registryId` + expiry. Directly parallels the existing credential-verification pipeline; extend the secretariat/committee tooling rather than build anew.
- **Public registry & verification:** `/verify/facility/[registryId]` returns live status (the badge is a lookup, not a static asset). Powers anti-fraud (§7).
- **Renewal / re-audit / revocation:** scheduled expiry, re-survey, one-click suspend with public status change and notification.
- **Referral rail:** from a member's dashboard / a Second Opinion case, route to an affiliated facility; log `FacilityReferral` for attribution and billing.

### 9.3 Surfaces
- **Public:** `/facilities` directory (filter by tier, state, specialty — clone the specialists page), facility showcase/microsite `/facilities/[slug]`, the verify page, a founding-affiliates feature.
- **Member:** "Register / manage my facility," application wizard, showcase editor, referral tools.
- **Admin/Committee:** assessor assignment, survey scoring, confer/revoke, COI disclosures, registry management, renewal calendar. Extend the existing admin + secretariat dashboards.

### 9.4 Integration points
- **Second Opinion** (`SecondOpinionCase`) → route follow-through to affiliated facilities.
- **Diagnostic network / ACO** → affiliates as preferred providers.
- **Structured EMR** → offer affiliates the EMR as a membership benefit (stickiness + data).
- **Billing** (Paystack NGN / Stripe FX) → affiliation fees, survey fees, referral fees.

### 9.5 Phasing the build
1. **MVP (Phase 0):** `Facility` + `FacilityAffiliation` models, admin confer/revoke, public registry + verify page, seed the cohort, basic showcase. *(Ships the seal + integrity spine.)*
2. **Directory + application wizard (Phase 2):** public `/facilities`, self-service Tier 0→2 application, assessor tooling.
3. **Rail + monetization (Phase 1/3):** referral routing + attribution, fees, HMO/insurer surfaces, Centres of Excellence + outcomes.

## 10. Top risks & mitigations (summary)

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Seal loses meaning if over-issued or self-claimed | Apply-and-confer, scarcity, independent survey, public revocations |
| Medico-legal liability for a bad affiliate | "Standard not guarantee" framing, indemnity, required insurance, rapid suspension, legal review |
| Conflict of interest (members own facilities) | Recusal, independent panels, logged disclosures, money/verdict separation |
| Competitor or government ships a rival seal | Move first on the seed cohort; position as the physician-governed complement to DHII; partner, don't compete, with HMOs |
| Standards credibility | Align to SafeCare/ISQua-type criteria; pursue external recognition for Tier 3 |

---

## 11. Recommended next steps

1. **Decide the frame:** DFC as a *trust & referral overlay* (recommended) — explicitly not a statutory regulator.
2. **Stand up governance first:** Accreditation & Standards Committee, COI rules, and the "standard-not-guarantee" liability language (legal review). *Before any badge ships.*
3. **Adopt a standards baseline:** engage SafeCare/PharmAccess for alignment or licensing rather than authoring standards.
4. **Pick the seed cohort:** 8–15 reputable member-owned facilities; expedited Tier-2 survey; publish as founding affiliates.
5. **Build the MVP spine:** Facility + Affiliation models, public registry + verify, admin confer/revoke, showcase.
6. **Turn on the rail:** route second-opinion/diagnostic/diaspora referrals to affiliates to prove ROI, then layer monetization.
